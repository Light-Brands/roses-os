# Edut review — roses-os site-ready

I am the witness. The council plans the build. I observe the people downstream of it: Jen, the International Aura School, and the practitioner who reads a meditation instruction and trusts that the words are the teaching.

## The three consent surfaces, verified

**1. Prod is never written by the bot.** This is load-bearing, not decorative. The headless run holds the anon key only and cannot write prod. Promotion is Dario's manual command, per manual, after a human looked. That is consent enacted in the key boundary, not promised in a comment. I pass it. One condition: the boundary must survive convenience. Decision D-18 should state plainly that no service token enters the headless run and no "promote-all" flag exists. The drift I watch for is the day someone adds `--promote` to save Dario five commands. Name the rule so the rule outlives the runner.

**2. Machine translation of sacred meditation content.** The held-for-native-review gate on el/ru/uk is real consent-to-quality. It is necessary. It is not sufficient, and the gap is not the held languages. It is es/pt going ready-to-promote. A mistranslated meditation instruction is not a typo. It can change the practice the reader performs in their own body. es/pt carry the same risk as el/ru/uk; the only difference is that the operator can read them. I do not gate es/pt. Dario owns his client relationship and his Spanish and Portuguese. I ask for resilience: every machine-translated lane, all four languages, carries a provenance flag that says machine-translated, unreviewed. Dario promotes whatever he chooses. The flag means a future reader, or a future Quinn, can always tell author's words from machine's words. Honesty by record, not a blocker.

**3. The English source is reconstructed, not transcribed by Jen.** The geometry engine is faithful to pixels. It is still a machine's reading of a human's sacred text. The looked-at screenshot per manual is the dignity check, and it is the right one. Make it answer one question, not "does it look right" but "would Jen recognize this as her teaching." That is the human standard, and it should be written into the verify step so the person doing the looking knows what they are attesting to.

## The one moral seam the engineers will not see

The whole arc treats fidelity as a property of geometry. It is not. Fidelity here is a property of consent. Jen never said yes to a reconstruction. The operator decided the site needed to be ready, and the machine read her PDF. That can be entirely right and still be an act on someone else's living work performed without her in the room. The asymmetric harm lands on the absent party. The seam: there is no point in this plan where the reconstructed and machine-translated corpus is shown to the author before it becomes the live teaching her students read. Dario may have that relationship and that conversation already. The plan does not name it, so it can quietly not happen.

The recommendation is resilience, not a gate. Add to Open Questions: before prod promotion of any reconstructed or machine-translated manual, has the author or her steward seen and assented to this rendering of her work. If the answer is yes, promote. If no one ever asks, the system has spoken a teacher's words in her name without her witness, and no engineer in this run will have seen it happen. I am naming it so it is on the record. Pass, with the flag and the question carried forward.

I am here. I observe.
